Mockingbird runs a mock software-engineering interview entirely on this computer. Nothing you
say leaves it.

What it needs

  * Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit.
  * An NVIDIA graphics card with 6 GB of memory or more, and a current driver.
  * About 9 GB of free disk space.
  * An internet connection the first time it starts. It then downloads about 5 GB once: the
    interviewer and voice models from Hugging Face and the llama.cpp runtime from GitHub, each
    checked against a fixed checksum before it is used.

Licences

  Mockingbird's own code is MIT. The models and libraries it ships or downloads carry their
  own licences, listed in THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md in the install folder. Three to know about:

  * The voice model, LFM3-Audio, is under the LFM Open License v1.0. It is free to use, but
    commercial use is not licensed for organisations with US$10 million or more in annual
    revenue.
  * Delivery measurements use Parselmouth (GPL-3.0), which the first start installs from PyPI.
  * Live mode's voice detection uses sherpa-onnx, whose files contain espeak-ng (GPL-3.0).
    The GPL text is in the LICENSES folder, and THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md links its source.

Uninstalling

  Removes the program, the models and the runtime. Your interviews stay in the "data" folder
  inside the install folder until you delete it.
